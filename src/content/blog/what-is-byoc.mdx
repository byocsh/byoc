# What is BYOC?

**Bring Your Own Cloud (BYOC)** is a deployment model where software runs inside *your* cloud account — not the vendor's. You get the convenience of a managed service while retaining full control over your data, network, and infrastructure.

## Why BYOC matters

Traditional SaaS means your data lives on someone else's servers. For many teams — especially those dealing with compliance, latency, or cost constraints — that's a problem.

BYOC flips the model:

- **Data stays in your account.** No cross-account data transfers. Your logs, metrics, and customer data never leave your VPC.
- **You control the network.** Private subnets, VPC peering, security groups — all yours.
- **Costs are transparent.** You see the actual cloud spend on your bill instead of opaque per-seat pricing.
- **Compliance is simpler.** SOC 2, HIPAA, GDPR — much easier when data doesn't leave your infrastructure.

## How BYOC works in practice

A typical BYOC deployment looks like this:

1. You grant the vendor limited access to your cloud account (usually via an IAM role)
2. The vendor deploys and manages the software within your infrastructure
3. All data processing happens inside your account
4. The vendor handles upgrades, monitoring, and maintenance remotely

The key difference from self-hosting is that **the vendor still manages the software** — you're not on your own for operations.

## BYOC vs. SaaS vs. Self-Hosted

| | SaaS | BYOC | Self-Hosted |
|---|---|---|---|
| Data location | Vendor's cloud | Your cloud | Your cloud |
| Operations | Vendor | Vendor | You |
| Network control | Limited | Full | Full |
| Setup effort | None | Low | High |
| Cost visibility | Opaque | Transparent | Transparent |

BYOC sits in a sweet spot: you get operational convenience *and* infrastructure control.

## The open source advantage

Many BYOC tools are open source, which means you can:

- **Inspect the code** that runs in your environment
- **Self-host first**, then move to managed BYOC when you need it
- **Avoid lock-in** — if the vendor disappears, you still have the software

## When should you choose BYOC?

BYOC makes sense when:

- Your compliance requirements restrict where data can live
- You need to keep traffic within your VPC for latency or security reasons
- You want managed operations but can't send data to a third party
- You're spending more on SaaS than the underlying cloud resources would cost
